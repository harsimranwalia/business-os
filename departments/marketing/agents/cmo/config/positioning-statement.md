# Positioning Statement — TEMPLATE

**This is a template. Fill it in for your business, then delete this block.**
The live copy lives at `$MKT_INSTANCE/agents/cmo/config/positioning-statement.md`
and is owned by the CMO; the copy under `$MKT_DEPT` is never edited and never
read at runtime.

This file is the anchor for every drafting run and for the biweekly positioning
audit. It has more downstream effect than any other file in the department: a
vague positioning statement produces competent content that sounds like anyone,
which is the exact failure the department exists to avoid. Update it when the
positioning genuinely changes — not to tweak the wording. Small edits here move
everything.

Empty is better than aspirational. A statement describing what the business
wishes it were will be faithfully executed by three agents for months.

---

## Who this is

> One or two sentences. Who the business is, what it actually does, and — this
> is the part most statements skip — **where the credibility comes from.** Not
> the claim; the source of the claim.

_Write it here._

## The distinction that matters

> Every good positioning statement contains one sharp "X, not Y" that a reader
> could disagree with. Generic statements have none, which is why they generate
> generic content: with nothing to be wrong about, every draft is safe.
>
> Examples of the *shape* (not of your positioning):
> "A practitioner whose work is the evidence, not an advisor who cites others'."
> "The team that runs it in production, not the one that specs it."

_Write it here._

## Target buyer

> Who is being sold to. Segment, size, and the situation they are in — the
> problem they already know they have, in the words they would use for it.
>
> This is a hypothesis, not scripture. The CMO tracks who actually engages and
> converts against this paragraph and proposes an update at M1 when they
> diverge, with evidence. It is never silently redrawn — a positioning
> statement that drifts without a decision is how a business ends up serving
> an audience nobody chose.

_Write it here._

> "not that" is easier for a drafting agent to check than "yes this."

## The positioning test

> Every piece must pass this, and it belongs in this file so there is exactly
> one copy of it. Two questions, both required. Keep them specific enough that
> a real draft can fail.

Does this reinforce **_[the identity named above]_**, and could it plausibly
attract **_[someone in the target buyer profile]_**?

## Filling this in

- **Source it from what already exists.** Real sales conversations, the words
  buyers actually use, closed deals, `../knowledge/business-profile.md`. Not
  from a positioning exercise done in a vacuum.
- **Write the "not" list first** if the rest is hard. It is usually easier to
  say what the business keeps being mistaken for, and the positives fall out of
  it.
- **Read it back as a filter, not a description.** The question is not "is this
  true?" but "would this reject a bad draft?" A statement that rejects nothing
  is decoration.
- **The audit is what keeps it honest.** Every other week the CMO scores the
  last eight shipped pieces against this file. If nothing ever fails, the
  statement is too loose — that is a finding, not a clean bill of health.
